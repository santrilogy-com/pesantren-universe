How it was made

The brief given to 1DesignTool's “3D object” template:

A tall, lean man in his 30s, about 1.90 m tall. Calm, distant expression. Wearing wrapped red cloth, beaded collar and sandals. Palette: red check, blue beads, white. Holding a long staff in the right hand. Realistic style. No text, logos, brand names or lettering anywhere.

Note: 116,411 triangles in 10 skinned meshes, all bound to one 65-bone skeleton with exact mixamorig: names, including Thumb, Index, Middle, Ring and Pinky 1–4 on both hands (three.js loads them as mixamorigHips and so on). The face is sculpted at ordinary adult proportions and was rebuilt after review: a straight nose with a clear bridge, tip and separate nostrils, about as wide as the inner corners of the eyes; natural closed lips with a philtrum, the mouth a third of the way from the nose base to the chin and the lips just behind the nose-tip-to-chin line; smooth cheekbones; a defined jaw and chin; and calm, symmetrical almond eyes. scripts/face-measure.mjs prints these landmarks from the sculpt. Bones have identity rest rotations (world-aligned axes; the T-pose is carried by joint positions). Most of the library uses that convention, but a few characters ship Mixamo-style bone axes, so retarget with a rest-pose-aware retargeter (Blender, Godot BoneMap, Unity Humanoid, three.js SkeletonUtils) rather than copying raw quaternions. At most 4 influences per vertex, normalised. The staff is its own mesh weighted 100% to mixamorig:RightHand. Because the bind pose has palms down, the right hand rests palm-down on the staff's rounded top with the fingers hooked over it, and the staff stands on the ground (its foot at y = 0). It is rigid to the hand, so a clip that lowers the arm swings it out sideways; rotate it at the hand if a clip needs it upright. With the staff param off, the right fingers keep that rest curl. Eyes, teeth and ears are rigid on Head; the wrist bands are rigid on the forearms and the ankle bands on the shins; the collar and both shuka follow the bones of the body under them, and the waist shuka hands itself over to the thighs (97% at the hem). Skin that the cloth fully covers (thighs, most of the torso) is not modelled, so the shuka cannot be removed, and a lifted thigh cannot push through the cloth. All PBR maps are drawn in code at build time and embedded as 2048 px JPEGs in three atlases (skin, cloth, gear): base colour, tangent-space normal (glTF convention) and one occlusion/roughness/metalness image each. The close-cropped hair and the lip colour are painted into the skin maps, so there is no separate hair or lip material. The rig test (scripts/rig-test.mjs) poses the arms down 70°, forearms 90°, left thigh forward 45° and Spine1 twisted 20°. It found no non-finite vertices, kept 0.89–0.90× girth at the shoulders and 0.80–0.81× at the elbows, stretched no edge more than 2.59× (at the right shoulder), and kept the staff within 5e-16 m of the hand. Nine short edges fold inside the armpit and elbow creases (down to 0.06× length), which is normal for linear-blend skinning. glb-lint shows two heuristic warnings: role materials that share an atlas have identical factors, and the worn pieces nest inside the body's bounds. The kit has no realistic-character manifest, so it was staged with mannequin-figure's neutral studio rig. No text, logos or animation clips.

About the Maasai-Style Herder 3D model

Maasai-Style Herder is a free realistic 3D model from the Festival and world culture characters set, rigged with a 65-bone Mixamo-compatible skeleton. It measures 1.9 m × 1.9 m × 37 cm (W × H × D) at real-world scale and is built from 116,411 triangles across 10 meshes, with 11 named parts. Download the 8.7 MB GLB and drop it into three.js, React Three Fiber, Godot or Blender — free for commercial use, no attribution required.

Is the Maasai-Style Herder 3D model free for commercial use?
Yes. It is free under the 1DesignTool 3D library license: use it in personal and commercial work — games, apps, websites, renders, ads and client projects — and modify it as you like, with no credit required. What you can't do is re-sell or re-distribute the file on its own or inside a model pack.
What file format is it, and how big is it?
A single binary glTF 2.0 file (.glb), 8.7 MB: 116,411 triangles, 63,279 vertices and 8 materials, modelled in metres at 1.9 m × 1.9 m × 37 cm.
Is Maasai-Style Herder animated?
It ships without a baked clip, in its bind pose. It is rigged with a 65-bone Mixamo-compatible skeleton, so it takes retargeted animation.
Which engines and tools can open it?
Anything that reads glTF 2.0. three.js, React Three Fiber, Babylon.js, Godot 4 and Blender import the GLB directly; Unity and Unreal Engine load it through their glTF importers. The snippets above show the exact code for three.js, React Three Fiber, Godot and Blender.
How was the Maasai-Style Herder 3D model made?
Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) generated it inside 1DesignTool's 3D template from a written brief, then the design kit's checks verified the geometry and the GLB before it was published. You can make your own the same way with 1DesignTool.
